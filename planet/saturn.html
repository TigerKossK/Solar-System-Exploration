Saturn

Gas giant · The ringed jewel · Known since antiquity (rings first seen by Huygens, 1655)

Famous for its brilliant rings of ice and rock, Saturn is the second-largest planet and the least dense — it would float in water if a bath were big enough.

Atmosphere & magnetosphere

Atmosphere

About 96% hydrogen and 3% helium, with traces of methane and ammonia. Fierce equatorial winds reach roughly 1,800 km/h.

Magnetic field

A strong field, unusually well aligned with the planet's rotation axis.

Make-up

Composition

Hydrogen and helium; spectacular rings of ice, dust and rock.

Notable features

  • The bright ring system, spanning about 280,000 km yet only tens of metres thick.
  • A six-sided polar jet stream — Saturn's 'hexagon'.

Fun facts

  • Saturn is the least dense planet — it would float in water if you had a big enough bath.
  • Its rings are made mostly of water ice and may be only 100–200 million years old.
  • The moon Titan has a thick atmosphere and lakes of liquid methane.
  • Enceladus shoots geysers of water ice into space from a hidden ocean.
  • A persistent six-sided jet stream circles Saturn's north pole.
