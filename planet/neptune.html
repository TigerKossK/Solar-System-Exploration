Neptune

Ice giant · The windswept deep · Johann Galle, 1846 — found within 1° of Le Verrier's mathematical prediction

The most distant planet, Neptune is a deep-blue ice giant with the fastest winds in the Solar System, found by mathematics before it was ever seen.

Atmosphere & magnetosphere

Atmosphere

About 80% hydrogen, 19% helium and 1.5% methane. Methane deepens its blue; winds top 2,000 km/h — the fastest of any planet.

Magnetic field

A tilted, off-centre field, inclined about 47° from the rotation axis.

Rings

Five faint rings of dark dust, named after astronomers such as Adams and Galle.

Make-up

Composition

Water, methane and ammonia ices; methane gives its vivid blue colour.

Notable features

  • The Great Dark Spot — an Earth-sized storm seen by Voyager 2 in 1989.

Fun facts

  • Neptune was predicted by mathematics from Uranus's orbit, then found within a degree.
  • It has the fastest winds in the Solar System — over 2,000 km/h.
  • Neptune radiates more heat than it receives from the distant Sun.
  • Its moon Triton orbits backwards and has active nitrogen geysers.
  • Neptune completed its first orbit since discovery only in 2011.

Exploration

Voyager 2 1989 The only visit — revealed the Great Dark Spot and Triton's geysers.
Hubble Telescope 1994– Tracks the birth and death of its giant storms.
